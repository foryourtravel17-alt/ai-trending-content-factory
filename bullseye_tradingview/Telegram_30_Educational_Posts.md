# Bullseye Theory — 30 Telegram Educational Posts

Use one post per day. Add an illustrative chart only if it is labelled historical/non-live. These posts are educational content, not trade calls. Do not append live prices, entry/exit instructions or profit promises.

## Day 1 — One Candle Is Not a Trend
A candle shows open, high, low and close for one period. It is a record of what happened—not a promise about the next candle. Zoom out before describing direction.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 2 — Read the Swing Sequence
Higher highs and higher lows can describe upward structure; lower highs and lower lows can describe downward structure. A single swing rarely tells the whole story.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 3 — Neutral Is a Valid Answer
Not every chart deserves a bullish or bearish label. When the Scope rule is unclear, FLAT means wait rather than inventing certainty.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 4 — What Is SCOPE?
Bullseye's first pillar sets context before any trigger: previous completed H4 EMA 50 vs EMA 200 for FX/crypto, or Daily context for stocks. Moving averages lag and can fail.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 5 — EMA Is a Tool, Not a Forecast
An EMA smooths past prices. It can help describe context, but it cannot know the future and may whipsaw in a range.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 6 — The Pullback Zone
The prototype's blue zone is EMA 21 plus/minus 0.5 ATR(14). Price must revisit the zone; chasing a move far away breaks the written process.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 7 — ATR Measures Movement
ATR describes recent price range, not direction. When volatility changes, the Bullseye band changes too. Always know which timeframe the ATR comes from.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 8 — Pattern Names Need Rules
Two people may define an engulfing candle differently. Bullseye uses a specific body-overlap test and a 55% body threshold; write rules before reviewing results.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 9 — A Wick Is Not Magic
A long wick can show a rejection during that candle, but it cannot prove that the level will hold. Location, context and the completed close still matter.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 10 — Trigger Means Conditions Agree
A Bullseye candidate requires Scope, zone interaction, a qualifying candle pattern, the required close and Guardrails. One attractive candle alone is not enough.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 11 — Wait for the Candle Close
An unfinished candle can change shape before it closes. The prototype uses confirmed chart candles and previous completed higher-timeframe data to reduce repainting risk.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 12 — SHIELD Comes Before Reward
The stop reference is set from the zone and ATR rule. Define invalidation first; do not choose a position size before understanding the risk distance.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 13 — What Is 1R?
R is the original risk distance from entry reference to stop reference. It is a measuring unit, not a forecast and not a guaranteed amount of profit.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 14 — A 2R Target Is Not a Promise
A planned target twice the risk distance does not mean price will reach it or that a strategy has positive expectancy. Outcomes need real testing.

## Day 15 — Partial Exit Basics
The current rule hypothesis references a 50% partial at 1R and the remaining target at 2R. The original stop stays in place at 1R in this version.

## Day 16 — Why Position Size Comes Last
Position size depends on stop distance, contract value, account currency, spread and slippage. If an input is unknown, do not guess a lot size.

## Day 17 — One Percent Is a Plan, Not a Shield
A planned 1% risk budget helps structure decisions but does not guarantee a maximum loss of exactly 1%; gaps and slippage can make losses larger.

## Day 18 — FLAT Saves You From Forcing It
A disciplined method needs a no-setup outcome. Waiting for defined conditions is different from trying to predict every move.

## Day 19 — Beware of Hindsight
If you move a swing point or change a candle definition after seeing what happened next, the test is no longer a fair test of the original rules.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 20 — Backtest With Costs
Historical testing should include spread, commission, slippage and correct contract settings. A cost-free test can exaggerate results.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 21 — Sample Size Matters
One or two trades do not establish an edge. Record all qualifying cases over a defined period, including losses and sideways markets.

## Day 22 — Out-of-Sample Means Untouched
Tune rules on one period, then evaluate them on later data that was not used to create the rules. This helps expose overfitting; it still cannot guarantee future performance.

**Reminder:** Educational content only—not financial advice. Historical charts are not live signals.

## Day 23 — The Journal Records Process

## Day 26 — Automation Has Boundaries
GitHub can store code and run scheduled tests. It cannot prove Pine compiles unless TradingView's compiler accepts the file, and it cannot press Publish in your logged-in account.

## Day 27 — Indicator vs Strategy
An indicator draws information on a chart. A strategy script simulates entries/exits in historical data. Neither one guarantees that a live broker order will behave identically.

## Day 28 — Repainting, Simply
Repainting means a result can change after it first appears. Confirmed candles and prior completed higher-timeframe values reduce common causes, but every script still needs careful review.
